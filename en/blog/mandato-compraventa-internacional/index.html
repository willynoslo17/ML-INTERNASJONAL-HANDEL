How an international sales mandate is built

Introducción

Before seeking suppliers or buyers, the parties usually define the scope of an international sales mandate. That document or agreement is not the sale itself; it delimits what the intermediary may do and under what conditions. This article describes, in general and educational terms, the elements that are habitually clarified.

It is not a contract model nor a guide to results. Each operation has its particularities and the parties must formalize their agreements with the support of professionals when they consider it necessary.

Elements that a mandate usually specifies

A clear mandate reduces misunderstandings. It usually addresses the goods, the market, the scope of the search and the limits of the intermediary’s action.

Goods and target market

It is indicated precisely what is sought or offered: description of the goods, technical or quality specifications, approximate quantities and possible origin, certification or cold-chain requirements. The market or markets of interest are also specified —for example, exports from Peru to Norway or the reverse direction— and whether known access restrictions exist.

Scope and limits of the action

The mandate defines whether the intermediary only identifies counterparties, whether it may also transmit offers or whether it intervenes in logistical coordination. It sets what decisions remain reserved to the principal (final price, acceptance of conditions, designation of carrier) and up to where the authorization reaches. That delimitation avoids the intermediary assuming responsibilities that correspond to the buyer or the seller.

Documentation and commercial conditions

The parties usually anticipate what documents will be necessary: commercial invoice, packing list, origin or phytosanitary certificates, and transport documents. It is also indicated how offers will be communicated and who validates the information before the operation advances.

Incoterms and responsibility

Incoterms 2020 distribute costs and risks between buyer and seller. The mandate may indicate preferences (for example, CIP or DAP in perishable products), but the definitive choice corresponds to the parties to the sale. It is useful that the mandate makes clear who will be the importer or exporter of record and who coordinates with the authorized customs representative.

From definition to the search for counterparties

Once those elements are defined, the intermediary may start the search. Consistency between what was agreed in the mandate, the offers received and the subsequent documentation facilitates the process. Diligence on the counterparty —company identity, data consistency and required licenses— forms part of usual practice before advancing toward a formal agreement.

Descargo

Informative and general text. It does not constitute a mandate model nor legal or commercial advice. The parties must formalize their agreements according to their concrete situation and with the support of authorized professionals. Search results or market-access conditions are not guaranteed.

Is a mandate a sales contract?
No. The mandate delimits the action of the intermediary; the sale is formalized between buyer and seller.
What must remain clear about the goods?
Description, specifications, approximate quantities, market and possible origin or certification requirements.
Who decides the Incoterm?
The parties to the sale. The mandate may indicate preferences, but does not replace the agreement between buyer and seller.
