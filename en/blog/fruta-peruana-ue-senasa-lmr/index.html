Peruvian fruit to the EU: SENASA certificate, MRL and traceability

Introducción

The export of Peruvian fruit (avocado, blueberries, asparagus, among others) to the European Union requires compliance with phytosanitary requirements, pesticide residue requirements and traceability. The usual starting point is the phytosanitary certificate issued by SENASA. The EU is governed, among other rules, by Regulation (EU) 2019/2072 on plant health.

This article describes the general concepts. It does not invent maximum residue limits (MRL), does not copy lists of treatments from the United States or from other destinations as if they were applicable to the EU, and does not offer personalised advice.

Certificado fitosanitario de SENASA

Base para la entrada en la UE

SENASA issues the phytosanitary certificate that accompanies the shipment. This document certifies that the lot complies with the phytosanitary requirements of the country of destination according to the inspection and the treatments carried out at origin. For the EU, the certificate must be consistent with the requirements of Regulation (EU) 2019/2072 and with any specific measures for the product and the origin.

The certificate is presented at the EU border control point together with the commercial and transport documentation. The authorities may carry out documentary, identity and physical controls according to the frequency in force at that moment (see the article on phytosanitary controls of 2026).

Límites máximos de residuos (LMR) y etiquetado

Trazabilidad hasta packing y productor

The EU sets maximum residue limits for active substances. These limits are published in official databases and may be updated. Concrete values are not reproduced here because they change and because this text does not intend to be a reference list. The exporter and the importer must verify the MRL applicable to the product and to the date of the shipment.

In addition to MRL compliance, labelling in accordance with EU regulation and traceability of the lot to the packing centre and the producer are required. Traceability allows, in the event of an alert or a control, to identify the origin and the practices applied.

Diferencia entre envío a la UE y a Noruega

A shipment destined for an EU Member State is not equivalent to a shipment destined for Norway. Norway is not part of the EU customs union; it applies its own import regulation, taxes (MVA) and controls. Although free trade agreements (EFTA-Peru) exist that may affect tariffs, phytosanitary and residue requirements must be checked separately.

Descargo

General informative text. It does not constitute personalised phytosanitary, customs or commercial advice. The MRL, the permitted treatments and the exact requirements must be verified in the official sources of the EU, of SENASA and of the country of destination on the date of the operation.

Which certificate is usually mentioned?
The phytosanitary certificate issued by SENASA (Servicio Nacional de Sanidad Agraria del Perú) is the starting document for the export of fruit to the EU.
Are concrete MRL published here?
No. Maximum residue limits are set by the EU and may vary by active substance and product. They must be consulted in the official databases.
What does traceability imply?
Being able to trace the lot to the packing and the producer, in accordance with the requirements of the EU and of the country of origin.
Is exporting to Norway the same as to the EU?
No. Norway is not part of the EU customs union and applies its own phytosanitary and customs regulation.
