CBAM 2026: what it is and which goods it usually affects

Introducción

The Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) of the European Union entered its definitive application period on 1 January 2026, as recorded by EY TradeWatch (May 2026) and other subsequent sources. Until then the mechanism was limited to quarterly reports of embedded emissions. From that date the imports of certain goods into the EU generate the obligation to declare emissions and, in due course, to surrender certificates whose price is linked to that of the EU emissions trading system.

This article explains the concept in general terms, the goods that sources usually mention and the nuances that appear in the Norwegian debate. It does not constitute legal, tax or customs advice. Companies must verify the concrete situation with the competent authorities and with their authorised customs representatives.

Qué es el CBAM en su periodo definitivo

Del reporte al certificado

During the transitional period (October 2023 to December 2025) EU importers submitted reports of embedded emissions without purchase or surrender of certificates. From 1 January 2026 the definitive regime requires, among other elements, the status of authorised declarant for imports that exceed certain mass thresholds, the annual declaration and, later, the surrender of certificates corresponding to the declared emissions.

Sources place the sale of certificates on the central platform from 1 February 2027 and the first annual declaration (corresponding to 2026 imports) around 30 September 2027. These dates are cited as they appear in the publications; any subsequent change must be checked in the official acts.

The price of the certificates is linked to the auctions of the EU ETS. Concrete price figures are not reproduced here because they vary by quarter and because this text does not offer calculations or estimates.

Mercancías a las que suele afectar según fuentes

Hierro, acero, hidrógeno y electricidad

Sources habitually cite iron and steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, hydrogen and electricity. Some proposals for scope extension (still in legislative process at the time of the publications consulted) contemplate certain products derived from steel and aluminium. The precise list and the exclusions depend on the consolidated text of Regulation (EU) 2023/956 and its amendments.

For an exporter outside the EU or the EEA, the starting point is the tariff classification of the good (HS code) and the non-preferential origin. The origin determines whether the good is covered or not. Goods originating in Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein and Switzerland are outside the scope of the EU CBAM according to Annex III of the regulation, according to sources that cite the legal text.

Umbral y declarante autorizado

Matices en Noruega

Several sources mention a threshold of 50 tonnes of CBAM goods per importer and calendar year (with exceptions for electricity and hydrogen). Below that threshold the status of authorised declarant is not required, although other registration or information obligations may exist. This threshold is cited only because it appears recurrently in the 2026 publications; it is not asserted that it is the only criterion or that it remains unaltered.

In Norway, KPMG and official material record the debate on an identification number linked to EU acts, including the CBAM, in the framework of legislative proposals (including Prop. 60 LS and references to Prop. 95 LS in the period 2025-2026). The objective that appears in Norwegian sources is full application from 1 January 2027. It is not asserted that the Norwegian CBAM is identical to that of the EU or that it is already in force with the same obligations.

Companies that import or export potentially covered goods must consult their authorised customs representative and the authorities (in the EU, the competent authority of the Member State; in Norway, the designated entities). Descargo

From when does the definitive period of the CBAM apply in the EU?
According to multiple sources, including EY TradeWatch of May 2026 and other subsequent publications, the definitive period began on 1 January 2026, after a reporting-only period.
Which goods are usually mentioned?
Sources cite iron and steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, hydrogen and electricity, among others. The exact list depends on the act in force at each moment.
Does it apply the same in Norway as in the EU?
It is not asserted that it is identical. Norwegian sources (including KPMG and official material) record the debate and the preparation of a own framework, with a target of full application in 2027 according to some proposals.
Who is the declarant?
The concept of authorised declarant appears in the EU legislation for those who import the covered goods above certain thresholds. The concrete verification corresponds to each operator and to the authorities.
