Authorised customs representative in Norway: functions and limits

Introducción

In the Norwegian customs system the authorised customs representative is the figure that, in practice, submits the declaration and carries out other customs formalities on behalf of the owner of the goods. It is not the same as the owner of the goods nor as the carrier. This distinction is relevant for any company that imports or exports to or from Norway.

ML Internasjonal Handel presents itself as a trade intermediary that coordinates export, international logistics and documentation, and whose clearance is carried out through an authorised customs representative. This article explains the concept of that role without attributing to the company functions that it does not declare.

What an authorised customs representative is

An authorised customs representative is a person or entity authorised to act before Tolletaten on behalf of another company. It may submit the customs declaration, send digital notifications in Digitoll and manage the supporting documentation. Its action is based on a mandate or agreement with the owner of the goods.

Not all companies can act as representatives. Authorisation implies compliance with requirements established by Tolletaten. Those who are not registered in Norway may resort to an authorised representative to fulfil the digital obligations.

Difference between owner, carrier and representative

Legal responsibility versus practical action

The owner of the goods (importer or exporter according to the direction of the flow) is legally responsible for the declaration and for the accuracy of the data on the goods, the origin and the value. The carrier (or the driver of the means) is legally responsible for the notification and disclosure of transport information.

The customs representative acts in practice on behalf of the owner. It may send the transport notification if the carrier entrusts it, submit the declaration and link both pieces of information. Its intervention does not transfer the final legal responsibility of the owner of the goods, unless the contractual agreement establishes otherwise in civil aspects.

Limits of the representative’s role

The representative is not the customs authority nor does it unilaterally decide the tariff classification, the preferential origin or the customs value. Those elements are provided by the owner of the goods or are determined according to the applicable rules. Nor does it replace the sales contract or the chosen Incoterm.

A representative does not guarantee that a shipment will not be selected for control nor that the crossing will be automatic. Its function is to facilitate the fulfilment of the formal obligations correctly and on time. Any doubt about the scope of the authorisation of a specific representative is resolved by consulting Tolletaten or the representative itself.

Descargo

This article is informative and of a general nature. It does not constitute legal, tax, customs or commercial advice. ML Internasjonal Handel is not a customs agent, authority or law firm. Clearance is carried out by an authorised customs representative.

Who is the legal person responsible for the customs declaration in Norway?
The owner of the goods (importer). In practice it may be submitted by an authorised customs representative.
Is the authorised customs representative the same as the carrier?
No. The carrier is responsible for the notification and disclosure of transport information; the representative acts on behalf of the owner of the goods for the declaration and other customs formalities.
Can any company act as a customs representative?
It must be an authorised representative. The specific requirements are consulted at Tolletaten.
Does ML Internasjonal Handel act as a customs representative?
No. According to its own site, clearance is carried out by an authorised customs representative.
