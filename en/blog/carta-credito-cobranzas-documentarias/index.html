Letter of credit and documentary collections

Introducción

In international trade instruments are used that link payment to the presentation of documents. Two of the most usual are the documentary credit (letter of credit) and documentary collection. This article describes their general functioning, without recommending one over the other or inventing commissions or deadlines.

Documentary credit or letter of credit

In a documentary credit, a bank undertakes to pay the beneficiary (normally the seller) if the latter presents the required documents and these prove conforming. The commitment is of the bank, not of the buyer, provided the terms of the credit are met.

The Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP 600) of the International Chamber of Commerce, 2007 revision (Publication No. 600), entered into force on 1 July 2007. They apply when the text of the credit expressly indicates that it is subject to them. The credit may modify or exclude articles.

Documents and bank examination

The bank examines the documents (invoice, transport document, certificates, etc.) against the terms of the credit. It does not verify the physical goods. Consistency between the documents is essential: description, quantities, Incoterm and parties must coincide.

Documentary collection

In documentary collection, the seller delivers the documents to its bank, which transmits them to the buyer’s bank. The buyer obtains the documents (and, in many cases, possession of the goods) against payment or acceptance of a bill. The bank acts as intermediary; it does not assume the payment commitment proper to a documentary credit.

Conceptual differences with the credit

In the documentary credit there exists a bank commitment conditioned on conforming documents. In collection, payment depends on the will and solvency of the buyer. Both instruments rely on commercial and transport documents, but their risk structure is different.

Relation with commercial documentation

The commercial invoice, the packing list and the transport document must be consistent with each other and with what is required by the credit or the collection. Any discrepancy may prevent payment or delay the delivery of documents. The parties usually coordinate in advance what documents will be required and who issues them.

Descargo

Educational and general text. It does not constitute financial advice nor recommend a payment instrument. Concrete conditions are set by the parties and the banks. Consult financial entities and authorized professionals. The UCP 600 apply only when the credit indicates so.

Does the letter of credit always guarantee payment?
The bank pays if conforming documents are presented according to the terms of the credit. It does not examine the physical goods.
What are the UCP 600?
The Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits of the International Chamber of Commerce, 2007 revision (Publication No. 600). They apply when the credit expressly indicates so.
Does documentary collection imply a bank payment commitment?
No. The bank acts as intermediary in the presentation of documents; payment depends on the buyer.
