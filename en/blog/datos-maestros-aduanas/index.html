Quality of master data for customs: description, origin and value

Introduction

Master data are the permanent or semi-permanent information that a company maintains on its products, suppliers and customers. In the customs field they include the description of the goods, the HS code, the origin, the value, the weight and other relevant characteristics. Their quality determines to a large extent the fluidity of clearance.

With the entry into force of Digitoll, the requirement of correct data is advanced to the moment prior to the border crossing. Analyses by KPMG Norge and by EY TradeWatch (May 2026) underline that commercial compliance depends increasingly on systems and on the quality of the data (HS, origin, value).

What are customs master data

A complete product sheet for customs contains, as a minimum, a precise and non-generic description, the correct HS code or goods number, the country of origin, the unit value and the units of measure. These data must be coherent with the commercial invoice, the packing list and the declaration.

The origin may be the non-preferential origin or the preferential origin according to the applicable agreement. The value must correspond to the customs value according to the applicable rules. Any discrepancy between internal systems and the documents presented to customs generates friction.

Digitoll and the advance requirement of correct data

Digitoll requires that the transport and goods information be available before or at the latest upon crossing the border. KPMG Norge describes how this digitisation advances the need for correct data. If the product sheet is incomplete, the customs representative or the importer cannot generate in time a coherent notification or declaration.

EY TradeWatch (May 2026) points out that commercial compliance depends increasingly on the quality of the master data and on the systems that manage them. A generic description (“machinery parts”) does not allow a correct classification nor an efficient risk assessment.

Consequences of an incomplete product sheet

An incomplete sheet may prevent the transport notification from being correctly linked with the declaration, delay tariff classification, prevent the claim of preferential origin or generate requests for clarification by Tolletaten. In the Digitoll environment, where transports with complete and advance information are prioritised, deficient data increase the probability of delays at the border.

The responsibility for maintaining quality master data corresponds to the company owner of the goods. The customs representative works with the information it receives. Improving the quality of the data is an internal process of the company, not a function that can be fully outsourced.

No concrete software tools are recommended here. The relevant concept is the coherence and completeness of the information used to fulfil customs obligations.

What master data are especially relevant for customs?
Description of the goods, HS code, origin, value, weight and quantity, among others. They must be coherent between invoice, packing list and declaration.
Why does Digitoll increase the importance of master data?
Because it advances the requirement of correct data before the border. KPMG Norge and other analyses underline this anticipation.
Can an incomplete sheet delay clearance?
Yes. Incomplete or incoherent data hinder the linking of notifications, classification and risk assessment, and may generate delays.
Is any concrete software recommended?
No. This article explains the concept of data quality, it does not recommend tools.
