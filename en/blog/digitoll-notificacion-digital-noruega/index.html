Digitoll Norway: digital transport notification to customs

Introducción

Digitoll is the digital framework of Tolletaten (Norwegian customs) for managing the entry of goods. From 15 September 2026 companies must send Tolletaten the transport information digitally, at the latest when crossing the border. This obligation is called notification and disclosure of information (melde- og opplysningsplikt).

The objective is for customs to have data on what goods are arriving, by what means, where and when, in order to carry out a risk assessment before or at the moment of crossing. Early information increases the likelihood that the transport has been processed and, where applicable, that an automated crossing and automatic release to the chosen customs regime is authorised. It is not a guaranteed automatic process.

What Digitoll is and when the digital obligation applies

Digitoll modifies the way notification and disclosure obligations are fulfilled. Information on the transport and the goods must be sent digitally before or, at the latest, at the moment of the border crossing. The exact moment depends on the mode of transport; the detailed deadlines are consulted on the Tolletaten pages.

Stage of 15 September 2026

From that date the notification and disclosure must be done digitally. Tolletaten processes the information and carries out the risk assessment. During the initial period after 15 September 2026 customs prioritise guidance when they detect errors or omissions. The use of sanctions is limited in that phase, although in cases of repeated, deliberate or serious non-compliance an administrative fine may be imposed.

Relationship with the subsequent declaration

The declaration obligation changes in a later phase. From 1 March 2027 the declaration must also be ready when crossing the border and the direct transport scheme that allowed declaring up to ten days later ceases to apply. Digitoll and the declaration are distinct stages: the first focuses on transport and goods data for the risk assessment; the second on the formal declaration to customs.

Who is involved in the digital notification

The driver of the means of transport is legally responsible for fulfilling the notification and disclosure obligation. In practice this may be done by a customs representative or a transport agent. The importer (owner of the goods) is responsible for the declaration, which in practice is also usually submitted by a representative.

Carrier, importer and representative

The carrier provides the data of the means, the time and the place of arrival. The importer or its representative provide the description of the goods, the weight, the quantity and, where applicable, the declaration identifier. The different notifications (transport, master consignment and house) must be able to be linked by means of consistent references. If the references do not match, Tolletaten cannot link the information.

To send information directly through Digitoll, registration in the Enhetsregisteret with a Norwegian organisation number or NUF is normally required. Those who do not have it may fulfil the obligation by means of a customs representative registered in Norway.

Why data quality matters

The data must be accurate and complete. An incomplete description, an incorrect weight or a reference that does not match may prevent Tolletaten from processing the transport in time or from correctly linking the notifications. In that case the transport may experience longer processing times and delays at the border. Tolletaten prioritises, as far as possible, transports that have fulfilled the digital obligation through Digitoll.

Data quality also facilitates the risk assessment and reduces the likelihood of additional controls. An incomplete product sheet or packing list results in information that customs cannot use efficiently. Therefore, coordination between carrier, importer and representative before the crossing is a central operational element.

What information does the Digitoll Norway digital notification cover?
Data on the means of transport, the goods and the driver, sent digitally before or at the latest when crossing the border.
Who is responsible for sending the notification?
The driver of the means of transport is legally responsible, although in practice a customs representative may act.
Does the digital notification guarantee an automatic crossing?
No. It may facilitate an automated crossing if the information is processed in time and there are no reasons for control, but it is not automatic in all cases.
What happens if the data arrive late or incomplete?
There may be longer processing times and delays at the border. Tollvesenet prioritises, as far as possible, transports that comply.
