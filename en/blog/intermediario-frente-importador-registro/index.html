Intermediary versus importer of record

Introducción

In international trade several distinct figures participate. One of them is the intermediary or commercial agent who acts by mandate of a buyer or a seller. Another is the company that appears as importer or exporter of record before customs. Both may coincide in the same operation, but they are not the same.

This article describes, in general terms, the difference between who intermediates and who assumes formal responsibility before the customs authorities. The description is adjusted to intermediation models from Oslo in which the company coordinates export, logistics and documentation, while clearance is carried out through an authorized customs representative.

What is an intermediary in international trade

A commercial intermediary facilitates the relationship between a seller and a buyer. It may seek suppliers, present offers, coordinate logistics or gather documentation. It acts normally by virtue of a mandate or of an intermediation contract that delimits its scope.

Mandate and limits of the action

The mandate usually specifies what goods are sought, in which market, with what scope and under what conditions. It also sets what documents the intermediary may request or transmit and what decisions remain reserved to the principal. When the intermediary is neither the buyer nor the seller of the goods, it does not assume, by the mere fact of intermediating, the condition of importer or exporter of record.

Importer or exporter of record

The importer of record is the person or company that appears in the customs declaration as responsible for the importation. In exportation, the equivalent figure is the exporter of record. That company responds before customs for the accuracy of the declaration, the tariff classification, the origin and the declared value, among other elements.

Responsibility before customs

Customs responsibility falls on who appears in the declaration, not on who has intermediated the commercial operation. Therefore, in intermediation models it is usual that the principal or a designated third party appears as importer or exporter, while the intermediary is limited to coordinating information and documents. Clearance proper is carried out by an authorized customs representative.

How both roles relate in practice

In a typical intermediation operation from Oslo, the intermediary may identify a supplier in Peru, coordinate maritime or air transport and gather the commercial invoice and the packing list. The company that buys the goods appears as importer of record in Norway. The authorized customs representative submits the declaration. The intermediary does not replace either of those two figures.

Clarity about who each one is reduces documentary frictions. Master data —description of the goods, HS code, origin, Incoterm and value— must be consistent between the mandate, the invoice, the transport document and the declaration. That consistency is the responsibility of the parties that provide and validate the information, not of the intermediary by the mere fact of coordinating it.

Descargo

This text is informative and general. It does not constitute legal, fiscal, customs or commercial advice. It does not attribute to ML Internasjonal Handel the condition of importer of record or of customs agent. Clearance is carried out through an authorized customs representative, as described on the site. The rules may vary; consult official sources and authorized professionals.

Does the intermediary always appear as importer?
No. Whoever intermediates by mandate does not have to be the importer of record; the figure that responds before customs is the one that appears as importer or exporter in the declaration.
Can an intermediary submit the customs declaration?
Clearance is normally carried out by an authorized customs representative. The intermediary coordinates information and documents, but does not replace that figure.
What data must the intermediary have clear?
Identity of the parties, description of the goods, agreed Incoterms and who assumes customs and fiscal responsibility.
