Cold chain and risk transfer in fresh foods

Introducción

In the international trade of fresh foods —Norwegian salmon, blueberries, asparagus or Peruvian avocado— the maintenance of temperature is an essential logistical requirement. At the same time, Incoterms 2020 determine at what moment the risk of loss or damage of the goods is transferred from the seller to the buyer. This article combines both concepts. It does not invent temperature ranges or shelf-life periods, and does not choose an Incoterm as the best.

What the cold chain is in international trade

Salmon, blueberries, asparagus and avocado

The cold chain consists of keeping the goods within the agreed or required temperature range from origin to destination. In maritime transport reefer containers are used; in air transport, containers or packaging with temperature control; on road, refrigerated vehicles. Any interruption (cold break) can affect the quality and safety of the food.

The concrete temperature requirements depend on the product, the contract and, where applicable, the regulations of the destination country. This text does not list them.

Risk transfer according to the Incoterms

The term does not follow the temperature

Incoterms 2020 (published by the International Chamber of Commerce) establish, among other aspects, the point at which the risk of loss or damage is transferred from the seller to the buyer. That point does not “follow” the temperature nor does it shift because the goods are perishable.

According to the conceptual guide of Remø, a cold break halfway along the route falls on whoever bears the risk at that moment according to the agreed Incoterm. For example:

  • In a term of group C (such as CIP), the risk is transferred when the goods are delivered to the first carrier, even if the seller contracts and pays the main transport and the insurance.
  • In a term of group D (such as DAP), the risk is transferred when the goods are placed at the disposal of the buyer at the agreed place of destination, ready for unloading.

Insurance coverage, when it exists, is a contract distinct from the Incoterm. The insurance may or may not cover certain temperature risks according to the agreed clauses.

Practical implications for the intermediary

ML Internasjonal Handel acts as a trade intermediary and coordinates international logistics. In practice, clarity on the chosen Incoterm, the point of risk transfer and the responsibilities of each party regarding temperature control helps avoid disputes if a cold break occurs. The documentation (commercial invoice, packing list, temperature instructions) must be consistent with the agreed term.

No contract template is offered here nor is a specific Incoterm recommended. The parties must agree the term that best reflects the distribution of costs and risks they desire, and document it clearly.

Descargo

Conceptual text based on the principles of Incoterms 2020 and on general logistical guides. It does not constitute personalised legal or commercial advice. The concrete risk transfer depends on the agreed Incoterm and the circumstances of the shipment. Consult your advisers and the authorised customs representative.

What is the cold chain?
The set of measures to maintain the appropriate temperature of fresh or refrigerated foods during transport and storage.
Does the Incoterm determine who is responsible for a cold break?
The Incoterm determines who bears the risk of loss or damage on each leg of the journey. A cold break halfway along the route falls on whoever bears the risk at that moment, according to the conceptual guide of Remø and the principles of Incoterms 2020.
Are temperature ranges given here?
No. The ranges depend on the product and on the requirements of the buyer or the regulations. They are not invented.
Are CIP or DAP “better” for perishables?
The best Incoterm is not chosen. It is explained that the risk is transferred at the agreed point, regardless of the temperature.
