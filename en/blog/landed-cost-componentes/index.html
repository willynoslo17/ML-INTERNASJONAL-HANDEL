Landed cost: usual components, without invented tariffs

Introducción

The landed cost is the total cost of making the goods available to the buyer at the agreed destination point. It includes the price of the goods plus all the additional costs necessary to bring them to that point and to comply with the customs and tax obligations. This article describes the usual components conceptually. It does not invent freights, duties, tax rates nor does it offer a calculator.

What the landed cost is

Beyond the price of the goods

The price that appears on the commercial invoice is only the starting point. According to the agreed Incoterm, the seller or the buyer assume different legs of the transport and the insurance. To those costs are added, at destination, the duties, the taxes, the port or airport surcharges and, where applicable, the compliance costs (inspections, certificates, temporary storage).

The result is the landed cost. Knowing it allows the importer to compare offers from different origins and to plan cash flow, but the concrete calculation depends on the goods, the origin, the destination, the Incoterm and the applicable regulations.

Usual components

Duties, taxes and surcharges

The components that usually appear are:

  • Price of the goods (according to invoice).
  • International freight (maritime, air or land) up to the agreed point.
  • Transport insurance, when contracted.
  • Import duties, determined by the tariff classification (HS code) and by the origin (preferential or not).
  • Import taxes. In Norway, the import MVA is one of them. The rate and the base must be verified in the official sources of Skatteetaten and Tolletaten.
  • Surcharges (handling, THC, storage, etc.).
  • Compliance costs (phytosanitary controls, certificates, inspections).

No values are assigned here to any of these components. Duties and taxes change and depend on the specific goods.

Relationship with Incoterms and master data

The Incoterm determines which costs are included in the agreed price and up to what point the seller’s responsibility reaches. A CIP offer includes the main transport and the insurance up to the named destination; an EXW offer does not. That is why it is necessary to read the Incoterm together with the invoice to understand what remains to be added to the landed cost.

The correct tariff classification (HS code) and consistent master data are the basis for determining the applicable duty and for avoiding subsequent corrections. In destinations such as Brazil, with the move to the DUIMP, the quality of the master data acquires even greater relevance.

Descargo

Conceptual text. It does not constitute personalised tax, customs or commercial advice. No numerical examples with freights or duties are included. Tax rates, duties and surcharges must be verified in the official sources of the destination country and with the authorised customs representative at the time of the operation.

What is the landed cost?
It is the total cost of bringing the goods to the agreed destination point, including the price of the goods, transport, insurance, duties, taxes and other applicable surcharges.
Are examples with numbers included?
No. This article does not invent freights, duties or tax rates.
Does the import MVA in Norway form part of it?
Yes, the import value added tax (MVA) is one of the usual components when the destination is Norway. The rate and the taxable base must be verified in the official sources.
Does it offer a calculator?
No. No calculator or result for a specific product is offered.
