Incoterms 2020: what they cover and what they do not cover

Introducción

Incoterms are standardised rules of the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC) that are used in international sales contracts. The current version is Incoterms 2020, in force from 1 January 2020. According to the Oslo Chamber of Commerce, there are 11 terms. Their main function is to fix the point of delivery and the moment at which the risk passes from the seller to the buyer. They often also indicate who organises the main transport or the insurance.

They are not a complete contract nor a template for a customs declaration. Their correct application requires that the parties expressly incorporate them into the contract and understand what they cover and what they leave out.

What Incoterms 2020 are

Incoterms have been published by the ICC since 1936 and are updated periodically. The 2020 version replaced that of 2010. Among the relevant changes are the move from DAT to DPU, the increase in the level of insurance required in CIP and the possibility, in FCA, of obtaining an on-board bill of lading when the parties agree.

The 11 terms

Seven terms are for any mode of transport: EXW, FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU and DDP. Four are only for sea transport or inland waterways: FAS, FOB, CFR and CIF. Using a maritime term in air or road transport is incorrect and may generate disputes about the point of delivery and the transfer of risk.

What they cover: delivery and transfer of risk

Each term defines the place or point at which the seller fulfils its delivery obligation and, therefore, the moment at which the risk of loss or damage to the goods passes to the buyer. They also usually indicate who contracts and pays the main transport and, in some cases (CIF and CIP), who contracts the insurance.

For example, in FOB the risk passes when the goods are on board the vessel at the port of shipment. In CIP the risk passes when the goods are delivered to the first carrier, even though the seller pays the transport to the destination. These rules are contractual between seller and buyer; they do not modify the customs obligations of each party vis-à-vis the authorities.

What they do not cover

Incoterms do not by themselves replace the sales contract. They do not regulate the price, the payment conditions, the quality guarantees, the applicable law or the resolution of disputes. Nor do they decide in all cases who pays the tariff or the import taxes; that depends on the specific term (only DDP includes the payment of import duties by the seller) and on what the parties have agreed.

They also do not “follow the temperature”. In perishable goods the Incoterm fixes the point of transfer of risk, but the obligation to maintain the cold chain is regulated in the contract and in the transport instructions. An incorrect choice of term (for example, using FOB or CIF in an air shipment) does not by itself create the obligations that the parties believe they have transferred.

The Oslo Chamber of Commerce stresses the importance of applying Incoterms correctly. Any doubt about the interpretation of a specific term is resolved by consulting the official text of the ICC or professional advice.

Descargo

How many terms are there in Incoterms 2020?
Eleven. Seven are for any mode of transport and four are only for sea transport or inland waterways.
Do Incoterms decide who pays the tariff?
Not always. They mainly fix the point of delivery and the transfer of risk, and often who organises the transport or the insurance. Payment of tariffs depends on the specific term and the contract.
Does an Incoterm replace the sales contract?
No. It is a clause that is incorporated into the contract. The full contract regulates many other aspects.
From when are Incoterms 2020 in force?
From 1 January 2020. The parties must expressly refer to the 2020 version if they wish to apply it.
