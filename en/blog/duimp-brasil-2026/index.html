DUIMP in Brazil (2026): the move to pre-validated data in import

Introducción

Brazil is carrying out one of the most significant customs transformations of recent decades. According to the Maersk article «Brazil & Latin America 2026: Redefining the Rules of Trade» (15 April 2026), the gradual dismantling of Siscomex under Ordinance 165/2024 marks the move from a transaction-based corrective compliance model to a pre-validated data architecture model.

This article describes the conceptual change. It is useful both for European or Norwegian exporters selling to Brazil and for readers in Latin America. It does not invent additional deadlines or sanctions.

From the border correction model to pre-validated data

Ordinance 165/2024 and withdrawal of Siscomex

In the traditional model, a large part of the correction of data and documents was carried out at the moment of clearance at the border. With the DUIMP (Declaração Única de Importação), the emphasis shifts towards the prior validation of the master data. The Maersk source positions the DUIMP as mandatory for most imports towards March 2026.

Later sources have mentioned adjustments in the calendar for the definitive withdrawal of the DI. Therefore, the exact calendar must be checked in the official communications of the Siscomex Executive Committee and of the Brazilian authorities at the time of the operation.

What the DUIMP implies according to the source

Product catalogue and master data

According to Maersk, importers must register the SKUs in a product catalogue with standardised attributes defined by the government. In practice, clearance begins during the validation of the master data, not only upon arrival of the shipment.

This requires greater synchronisation between the importer’s ERP systems, the product catalogue, the tax engines and the customs declaration. Discrepancies that previously could be corrected at the border now have more immediate consequences in the flow.

The source also mentions other elements of the Brazilian regulatory environment of 2026 (such as the dual implementation of CBS/IBS), but this article focuses on the change in the declaration model.

Relevance for exporters from Europe and Norway

For a European or Norwegian exporter selling to a Brazilian importer, the quality and consistency of the data that are sent (description, tariff classification, origin, weights, etc.) acquire greater importance, because the importer uses them to feed their catalogue and their DUIMP declaration. An incomplete description or an incorrect classification can generate delays in the prior validation process.

Descargo

Information based on the Maersk article of 15 April 2026 and on later references regarding the calendar. It does not constitute personalised customs advice for Brazil. The exact deadlines, the catalogue requirements and the concrete obligations must be verified in the current official Brazilian sources.

What is the DUIMP?
The Single Import Declaration (DUIMP) is Brazil’s new import declaration system that gradually replaces the Import Declaration (DI) of Siscomex.
When is it positioned as mandatory?
According to the Maersk article of 15 April 2026, DUIMP is positioned as mandatory for most imports towards March 2026, within the framework of Ordinance 165/2024. Later sources mention possible calendar adjustments; the current official calendar must be verified.
What changes in practice?
The emphasis moves from correcting documents at the border to previously validating the master data (including the product catalogue with standardised attributes).
Are sanctions invented?
No. This article does not describe sanctions or deadlines additional to those cited in the source.
