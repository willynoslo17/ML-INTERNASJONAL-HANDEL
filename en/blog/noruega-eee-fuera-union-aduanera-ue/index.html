Norway in the EEA but outside the EU customs union

Introducción

Norway forms part of the European Economic Area (EEA) together with Iceland, Liechtenstein and the Member States of the EU. However, it does not form part of the EU customs union. Tolletaten treats Norway as an independent customs territory. This distinction is fundamental for any company that moves goods between a Member State of the EU and Norway.

Confusing the EEA with the customs union or with the Schengen area generates errors in the planning of customs formalities. This article clarifies the concepts without inventing tariffs or procedures.

EEA, customs union and Schengen: distinct concepts

The EEA is an agreement that extends the EU internal market to Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein in most sectors (goods, services, capital and persons), with notable exceptions such as agriculture and fisheries. It is not a customs union.

A customs union implies a common external tariff and the elimination of internal customs formalities. The EU is a customs union. Norway is not. Therefore, goods that cross the border between the EU and Norway are subject to customs formalities on both sides.

Schengen is an area of free movement of persons. Norway forms part of Schengen, but that does not eliminate customs formalities on goods.

Norway as an independent customs territory

Tolletaten administers its own customs territory. The rules of classification, origin, value and declaration procedures are the Norwegian ones, although in many aspects they are harmonised or influenced by those of the EU. A shipment that arrives in Norway from a Member State of the EU is treated, from the Norwegian customs point of view, as an import from an external territory.

Consequences for shipments from an EU Member State

A shipment from a Member State of the EU to Norway usually requires an export declaration in the EU and an import declaration in Norway. Transport operators and operational summaries (for example, explanations about Digitoll for trucks) confirm this usual practice.

The existence of the EEA may facilitate trade in many industrial products and reduce or eliminate tariffs under certain conditions, but it does not eliminate the need for declarations. The rules of origin, certificates and supporting documentation remain relevant when preferential treatment is claimed. For agricultural and fishery products specific regimes exist.

Any tariff figure or specific requirement must be verified in the official sources of Tolletaten and of the customs authority of the Member State of the EU of origin. Automatic preferences cannot be assumed.

Descargo

This article is informative and of a general nature. It does not constitute legal, tax, customs or commercial advice. Tariffs are not invented nor is it stated that a specific rule “already obliges” or “no longer obliges” without a source. ML Internasjonal Handel is not a customs authority.

Is Norway part of the EU customs union?
No. Norway is in the European Economic Area (EEA) but outside the EU customs union. Tolletaten treats it as an independent customs territory.
Does a shipment from the EU to Norway require an export declaration in the EU?
Normally yes, in addition to the import declaration in Norway. The detail depends on the specific case.
Does the EEA imply free movement of goods without customs formalities?
No. The EEA facilitates trade in many products, but does not eliminate customs formalities between the EU and Norway.
Does Schengen affect customs declarations?
Schengen refers to the free movement of persons. It does not eliminate customs formalities on goods.
