Preferential origin in the EFTA–Peru free trade agreement

Introduction

The free trade agreement between the States of the European Free Trade Association (EFTA/AELC: Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland) and Peru was signed in 2010 and entered into force for Norway on 1 July 2012. It allows, under the conditions provided, preferential tariff treatment for goods that comply with the rules of origin.

Not all goods originating in Peru obtain preference automatically. It is necessary to comply with the rules of Annex V and to evidence the origin in the manner provided. This agreement is distinct from the free trade agreement between the European Union and Peru; they must not be mixed.

The EFTA–Peru agreement

According to information from EFTA, the agreement was signed on 24 June 2010 in Reykjavik by the EFTA States and on 14 July 2010 in Lima by Peru. It entered into force on 1 July 2011 for Peru, Liechtenstein and Switzerland, on 1 October 2011 for Iceland and on 1 July 2012 for Norway.

It covers trade in goods, with specific provisions for industrial products, fisheries and processed agricultural products, in addition to rules of origin, customs facilitation and other chapters. Tolletaten maintains a specific page on the Norway–Peru agreement.

Rules of origin and proof of origin

The rules of origin are in Annex V. They determine which goods are considered originating and, therefore, may benefit from preferential treatment. They may be based on criteria of wholly obtained goods, change of tariff heading or value added, according to the product.

The proof of origin is usually an EUR.1 movement certificate or an origin declaration on the invoice, according to the conditions provided in the agreement. Without the correct proof, Norwegian customs do not apply preferential treatment, even if the goods materially comply with the rules.

It is not assumed here that any concrete goods obtain preference nor are tariffs indicated. Each shipment must be verified according to Annex V and the available documentation.

Difference with the EU–Peru agreement

The European Union has its own free trade agreement with Peru, negotiated in the framework of the Andean region. The rules of origin, the certificates and the procedures are distinct. Goods that comply with EU–Peru preferential origin do not automatically comply with EFTA–Peru origin, and vice versa.

For companies that operate both with the EU and with Norway it is necessary to manage two sets of rules and of proofs of origin. Confusing them may lead to preference being rejected in one of the territories.

Since when has the EFTA–Peru agreement been in force for Norway?
Since 1 July 2012. The agreement was signed in 2010.
Where are the rules of origin?
In Annex V of the agreement. The proof of origin is usually an EUR.1 certificate or an origin declaration, according to the case.
Does all Peruvian goods obtain automatic preference in Norway?
No. They must comply with the rules of origin of Annex V and be correctly evidenced. No tariffs are invented nor is universal preference assumed.
Is it the same agreement as that of the EU with Peru?
No. They are distinct agreements. They must not be mixed.
