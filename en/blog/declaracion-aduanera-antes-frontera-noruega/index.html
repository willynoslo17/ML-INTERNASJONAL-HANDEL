Customs declaration Norway before the border in 2027

Introducción

The digitalisation of Norwegian customs advances in phases. It is important to distinguish the stage of 15 September 2026, focused on transport data under Digitoll, from the stage of 1 March 2027, in which the customs declaration must also be ready when crossing the border and the scheme that allowed declaring up to ten days later disappears.

Confusing both dates generates incorrect expectations about what information must be available at each moment. This article explains the concept of each stage without describing detailed operational procedures, which must be consulted at Tolletaten.

Two distinct stages in customs digitalisation

15 September 2026: transport data

From that date companies must send Tolletaten digitally the transport information (notification and disclosure) before or at the latest when crossing the border. This information allows customs to know what goods are arriving, by what means and when, and to carry out a risk assessment. It is not the formal customs declaration.

1 March 2027: declaration at the crossing

From 1 March 2027 the declaration must be submitted before or at the latest when crossing the border. The direct transport scheme (direktekjøring) that allowed transporting the goods to the recipient and declaring up to ten days after the crossing ceases to apply. The possibility of declaring after the crossing when customs warehouses are used also changes.

Until that date it is possible, and advisable, to use Digitoll fully by also sending the declaration before the crossing and including the reference in the shipment notification. Operational sources (including summaries from Maersk Customs Services Norway and transport operators) confirm this sequence of dates.

What changes with the end of the direct transport scheme

The direct transport scheme had been a feature of the Norwegian system for decades. It allowed greater temporal flexibility for submitting the declaration. Its disappearance means that the complete information on the goods and the chosen customs regime must be available to Tolletaten at the moment of the crossing.

This brings the Norwegian process closer to more common practices in other customs territories, in which the declaration precedes or coincides with the crossing. It does not imply that all crossings are automatic or that controls disappear. Tolletaten can still select transports for physical inspection.

Roles and presentation of the declaration

The owner of the goods (the importer) is legally responsible for the declaration. In practice this is usually submitted by an authorised customs representative. The carrier remains responsible for the notification and disclosure of transport information.

The declaration is submitted through TVINN (for declarations) or NCTS (for transit). Digitoll acts as the notification and data linking layer. The different pieces of information must be able to be linked by means of consistent references. If there are doubts about the specific procedure, the reference source is Tolletaten.

Descargo

From when must the Norwegian customs declaration be ready when crossing the border?
From 1 March 2027, according to information from Tolletaten. Until that date the direct transport scheme may continue to apply under the prevailing conditions.
Does the Digitoll obligation of September 2026 already require the declaration?
No. The September 2026 stage refers to the digital notification and disclosure of transport information. The declaration at the crossing enters in a later phase.
Who submits the declaration?
The owner of the goods (importer) is legally responsible, although in practice it is usually done by an authorised customs representative.
Where is the exact procedure consulted?
On the official pages of Tolletaten (toll.no). Any operational doubt must be resolved there.
