Due diligence on the counterparty and the supplier

Introducción

Before committing to a supplier or a buyer in an international operation, companies usually carry out basic checks. This article describes, in a prudent and educational tone, what elements are reviewed frequently. It does not offer lists of warning signals nor guarantees that a control eliminates all risk. It also does not recommend databases, scorings or verification providers.

Identidad y existencia de la empresa

The first thing is to confirm that the counterparty exists as a legal entity and that the data it provides are coherent.

Registros y datos básicos

The legal name, the organization or registration number equivalent in its country, the domicile and the capacity to act are verified. In operations involving Norway or Peru, the corresponding public registers allow contrasting part of that information. The coherence between what the company declares and what appears in official registers is a usual element of review.

Coherencia documental y requisitos de la mercancía

The documents presented by the counterparty (offers, certificates, invoice samples) must be internally coherent and compatible with the goods described.

Licencias, certificados y origen

Depending on the product, phytosanitary certificates, of origin, of conformity or specific licenses may be required. The existence and validity of those documents, as well as the coherence of the declared origin with the applicable preferential agreements (for example, between EFTA and Peru), form part of the prior review. They do not replace the controls that the authorities will carry out at destination.

Claridad del mandato y de los Incoterms

When an intermediary intervenes, the mandate must make clear the scope of its action and who will be the importer or exporter of record. The Incoterms must be defined between buyer and seller so that costs and risks are distributed. That clarity reduces subsequent disputes about who had to contract the transport, the insurance or assume certain formalities.

Due diligence is a continuous process: what is checked before signing may require updating if the conditions or the goods change.

Descargo

Educational and general text. It does not constitute legal or commercial advice nor guarantees the elimination of risks. Each company must adapt the checks to its situation and consult authorized professionals when it considers it necessary.

Does a check eliminate all risk?
No. It reduces identifiable uncertainties, but does not guarantee the absence of subsequent problems.
What identity data are usually reviewed?
Legal name, organization number or equivalent, domicile and coherence with the documents it presents.
Who must carry out the due diligence?
Each party according to its role and risk appetite. The intermediary may facilitate information, but the decision corresponds to whoever commits.
