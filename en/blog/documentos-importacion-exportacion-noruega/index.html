Usual import and export documents in Norway

Introduction

Import and export to or from Norway require documentation that allows Tolletaten to identify the goods, their value, their origin and the transport. There is no single mandatory checklist applicable to all shipments. The necessary documents vary according to the goods, the country of origin, the Incoterm and the chosen customs procedure.

This article describes usual categories based on operational practices and on the technical possibilities offered by Tolletaten, without presenting them as an exhaustive or mandatory list.

Usual commercial documents

The commercial invoice and the packing list are documents that appear recurrently in the operational sheets of customs operators, such as Maersk Customs Services Norway. The commercial invoice identifies the parties, describes the goods, indicates the value and the Incoterm. The packing list details the content of each package, weights and dimensions.

The HS code (or the goods number of the Norwegian tariff) is not a document in itself, but must appear in the declaration and is usually included in the invoice or in the packing list. Its correct determination is the responsibility of the owner of the goods.

Origin, permits and transport documents

According to the case, certificates of origin (for example, EUR.1 or origin declaration when preference is claimed), sanitary or phytosanitary permits, import or export licences, and transport documents (bill of lading, air waybill or CMR) may be added.

These documents are not universal. A certificate of origin is only necessary when it is desired to benefit from a free trade agreement. A phytosanitary permit depends on the goods and on the rules applicable at the time of import. Waybills evidence the transport contract and may be relevant for valuation or for tracking.

Sending of supporting documentation to Tolletaten

Tolletaten allows sending supporting documentation by API. Among the types of documents that can be transmitted are invoices, permits, freight documents, certificates of origin and waybills. This possibility forms part of the digital environment of Digitoll and facilitates that the information is available to customs without the need to present physical papers in all cases.

The quality and coherence of these documents with the data of the declaration and of the transport notification are relevant. An invoice whose value or description does not match the declaration may generate requests for clarification or delays.

Disclaimer

This article is informative and of a general nature. It does not constitute customs advice or a mandatory checklist. The concrete documents depend on the case and must be verified with the authorised customs representative and with Tolletaten. ML Internasjonal Handel coordinates documentation; the clearance is performed by an authorised representative.

Is there a mandatory checklist of documents for all shipments to Norway?
No. The necessary documents depend on the goods, the origin, the Incoterm and the customs procedure. This article describes usual categories, not a mandatory list.
What documents appear frequently in operational sheets?
Commercial invoice, packing list and HS code are mentioned as usual documents in summaries of operators such as Maersk Customs Services Norway.
Does Tolletaten accept supporting documentation digitally?
Yes. Tolletaten allows sending supporting documentation by API (invoices, permits, freights, certificates of origin or waybills, among others).
Is the certificate of origin always necessary?
No. It depends on whether preferential treatment is claimed and on the applicable agreement.
